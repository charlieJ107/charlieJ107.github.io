Chattable Avatars explores how visitors can engage with cultural heritage through conversation with a digital character. The research brings together an interactive prototype and work with heritage professionals to understand where conversational AI can fit into an exhibition.

**[Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.14236/ewic/BCSHCI2025.10)** · [Open-access PDF](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/180401/1/91-Jiang-BCSHCI25.pdf) · [Project background](#project-background-and-public-coverage)

## Heritage through conversation

The GW4 collaboration, led by [Dr Yipeng Qin](https://yipengqin.github.io/) at Cardiff University, explored avatars for museums and archives alongside their ethical and social implications. Cardiff's related community project worked with the National Trust's Bath Assembly Rooms on interpreting Georgian Bath through interactive characters. Technical development and co-production informed the characters' appearance, personality and role in the visitor experience. The institutional pages below describe this wider research context.

## How the prototype works

The published prototype combines Unreal Engine 5 and MetaHuman with language and speech technologies. Its interaction follows a simple sequence:

1. A visitor holds a key to speak; speech recognition transcribes the recording.
2. A language model uses character context and conversation history to generate a reply.
3. Speech synthesis and facial animation let the character respond aloud.

See the [paper](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/180401/1/91-Jiang-BCSHCI25.pdf) for the implementation and workshop method.

## My contribution

As a Research Assistant at Cardiff University from **September 2023 to May 2024**, I built the initial Unreal Engine, LLM and ASR/TTS platform, implemented prompt and system orchestration for sustained conversation, and supported early studies within the GW4-funded collaboration. The prototype provided a platform for user studies and subsequent research.

## Research outcome

**Zhuoling Jiang**, Yipeng Qin and Daniel J. Finnegan (2025). *‘Chattable’ Avatars: Using LLMs to Power Visitor Engagement with Historical Persons*. BCS HCI 2025, pp. 91–102.

The paper reports a co-design workshop at Bath Assembly Rooms. Its qualitative findings examine trust, authority, social experience and location, suggesting a role for avatars in interpretive storytelling while highlighting the limits of factual reliability. These findings inform future exhibition design.
